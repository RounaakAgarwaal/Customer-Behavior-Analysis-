# Customer-Behavior-Analysis-

An end-to-end data analytics project exploring customer shopping behavior using transactional data from 3,900 purchases. The project covers data cleaning and feature engineering in Python, business-question analysis in SQL, and an interactive Power BI dashboard for stakeholder reporting.

## Project Overview

This project analyzes customer transaction data to uncover insights into spending patterns, customer segments, product preferences, and subscription behavior — with the goal of informing strategic business decisions around marketing, discounting, and customer retention.

## Workflow

CSV data → Python (cleaning & feature engineering) → MySQL (business analysis) → Power BI (dashboard & storytelling)

## Dataset

Rows: 3,900 customer transactions 

Columns: 18 

Format: CSV 

## Key fields

Demographics: Customer ID, Age, Gender, Location

Purchase details: Item Purchased, Category, Purchase Amount (USD), Size, Color, Season

Shopping behavior: Subscription Status, Shipping Type, Discount Applied, Promo Code Used, Previous Purchases, Payment Method, Frequency of Purchases, Review Rating

The raw `Review Rating` column had 37 missing values, which were imputed using the median rating per product category.

## Tech Stack

- **Python** (pandas) — data cleaning and feature engineering
- **MySQL** — structured querying for business analysis (via SQLAlchemy)
- **Power BI** — interactive dashboard and visualization
- **Jupyter Notebook** — analysis environment

## Business Questions Answered (SQL)

All queries are in `customer_behavior.sql`, run against the cleaned `customer` table:

1. Total revenue generated by male vs. female customers
2. Customers who used a discount but still spent above the average purchase amount
3. Top 5 products by average review rating
4. Average purchase amount: Standard vs. Express shipping
5. Average spend and total revenue: subscribers vs. non-subscribers
6. Top 5 products by discount usage rate
7. Customer segmentation into New / Returning / Loyal based on purchase history
8. Top 3 best-selling products within each category
9. Whether repeat buyers (>5 previous purchases) are more likely to subscribe
10. Revenue contribution by age group

## Key Findings

- **Revenue by gender:** Male customers generated roughly double the revenue of female customers ($157,890 vs. $75,191).
- **Discount-driven spend:** 839 customers used a discount while still spending above the average purchase amount, showing discounts aren't purely driving down basket size.
- **Top-rated products:** Gloves, Sandals, Boots, Hats, and Skirts led average review ratings (all above 3.75).
- **Shipping type:** Express shipping customers spent slightly more on average ($60.48) than Standard shipping customers ($58.46).
- **Subscriptions:** Non-subscribers accounted for far more total revenue ($170,436 vs. $62,645) simply due to volume (2,847 vs. 1,053 customers) — average spend per customer was nearly identical between the two groups.
- **Customer segments:** The majority of customers (3,116) fall into the "Loyal" segment, with far fewer New (83) or Returning (701) customers.
- **Repeat buyers:** Of customers with more than 5 previous purchases, the large majority (2,518) are non-subscribers, suggesting loyalty isn't currently converting into subscriptions.
- **Revenue by age group:** Fairly evenly spread across age groups, with Young Adults contributing the most ($62,143) and Seniors the least ($55,763).

## Dashboard

An interactive Power BI dashboard (`customer_behavior_dashboard.pbix`) summarizes the analysis with filters for subscription status, gender, category, and shipping type, alongside visuals for:

- Number of customers, average purchase amount, and average review rating (KPI cards)
- Customer split by subscription status
- Revenue and sales by category
- Revenue and sales by age group
